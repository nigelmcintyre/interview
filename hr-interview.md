# HR screening prep — Citco

## Q1: Walk me through your CV / Tell me about yourself

I'm a full-stack developer with about five years' experience, mostly across C#/.NET and Python.

At Propylon I work on a legislative document management system — a Python/Django application with JavaScript front ends, plus a legislative drafting Word add-in built in C#. So I spend a lot of my time on the seam between front-end authoring tools and backend asset management. Before that, my main experience was at Irish Life building .NET web apps and REST APIs for retail investment products, which is where I first worked in financial services.

Over the last two years, seeing the increase in AI usage and how Python has become the main language for building AI-related tools, I've been deliberately deepening my Python and backend work — both at my job and through personal projects like my sauna directory website, and more recently learning FastAPI with PostgreSQL backend design. It's my way of taking the document-systems experience I already have and building it in the modern Python/API stack I want to work in more of.

## Q2: Why Citco, why this role?

That's really why this role stood out — it sits right at the intersection of the three things I keep gravitating toward: Python backend work, AI and agentic development, and financial services. I'd like to do that on a real platform with a strong team, not just on my own project.

## Q3: Why are you leaving Propylon?

I'm looking to grow my skillset with new technologies in a new domain, and to work on AI projects as part of my day-to-day role rather than just on the side. I've already been building that direction myself: an AI-augmented trading setup screener, and tinkering with AI orchestration tools like n8n.

## Suggestions

- **Typo pass done** — fixed "Irish ife" → "Irish Life", "befome" → "become", "i'M" → "I'm", "me skillset" → "my skillset" in the version above.
- **"openclaw"** — dropped from Q3. I don't recognize this as a real AI orchestration tool (n8n, yes; "openclaw," not that I can find). If it's a real tool you use, put it back in — but don't name-drop something in an interview you can't describe in one follow-up sentence.
- **Q3 — "looking for a new challenge"** is a cliché line every interviewer has heard a hundred times; it doesn't cost you anything but it doesn't add anything either. The rewrite above leans on what you actually have evidence for (the trading screener, n8n) rather than the generic framing — worth practicing saying it that way so it doesn't feel like reading a script.
- **Q3 — don't let it sound like you're running from Propylon.** Current phrasing is fine (forward-looking, not a complaint), just keep the tone that way if they probe further — "new challenge" not "problems there."
- **Q2 could name Citco/MOS specifics** if you want to show you've researched them — e.g. "...and specifically a domain like treasury/collateral where the stakes for getting it right are real" — but the current answer is a safe, solid default and you don't need to force it.
- **Salary** — a single number (€80k) invites a straight yes/no rather than a negotiation. If you want room to move, consider stating it as "around €80k, flexible depending on the full package" — your call whether you want that flexibility signaled at this stage.
- **Notice period vs. start date** — you've listed both as 30 days, which is consistent, but expect them to just ask one and infer the other; no need to volunteer both unprompted.
